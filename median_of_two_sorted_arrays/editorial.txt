A modified binary search algorithm. For each array, keep track of a possible range with a variable for low and high.
The starting range is the entire array, for both arrays. Like binary search, grab the elements of the middle of both ranges.
Call the middle elements x and y, both arrays are sorted, x and y are greater than or equal to the elements to their left.
Compare x and y, suppose y = max(x, y). Let ix and iy be the indices of x and y, respectively.
Now, we know y is greater than or equal to ix+iy+1 elements, since y >= x, and thus also all elements preceding x.
If ix+iy+1 is more than half the combined size of both arrays, then y cannot be the median.
In which case, we reduce the possible range of the array that y came from, to the lower half.
Otherwise, if ix+iy+1 is at least half, x cannot be the median, reduce its possible range to the higher half.
Similar story goes if x = max(x, y), at each step, one of the possible ranges is halved.
Everything outside of the possible ranges is either before the median or after the median.
Repeat until one of the ranges is empty. Then, the median is at index half minus the lower bound of the empty range.
Either that, or it is at the lower bound of the empty range.
If the combined size is even, search both arrays for the other number and take their arithmetic mean.
Note that this algorithm appears to run in log(m)+log(n) time, and the question requires log(m+n) time.
However, log(m)+log(n) = log(mn) <= 2log(m+n) = log(m^2+2mn+n^2) for m >= 1 and n >= 1.
Therefore log(m)+log(n) is in O(log(m+n)).
